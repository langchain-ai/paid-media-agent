# Benchmarks

Published benchmarks (industry CPC, CTR, CPL, ROAS tables from platforms and agencies) are
directional context, not targets. They mix industries, geographies, funnel stages, bidding
strategies, and attribution settings, and they age quickly.

## How to use one

- Compare a metric to its own history first: the same entity, same window length, prior period.
  That comparison inherits the account's attribution, currency, and seasonality.
- Cite an external benchmark only when the user asks whether a figure is "good" and no configured
  goal exists. Say which benchmark, roughly when it was published, and why it may not apply.
- Prefer a range to a point. A benchmark quoted as one number implies precision it does not have.
- Never turn a benchmark into a pass or fail verdict in a report. Reports carry configured goals
  or label the read as directional.

## What tends to differ between platforms

- Search intent (Google) usually converts at a higher rate and a higher CPC than social feeds.
- Social platforms optimize toward their own conversion events; a "conversion" on one platform is
  not the same event as on another (see metrics-and-attribution.md).
- Professional-audience platforms (LinkedIn) carry higher CPCs and CPLs; judge them on pipeline
  quality when a warehouse or CRM is connected, not on CPL alone.
- Newer or smaller platforms (Reddit, OpenAI Ads) report fewer metrics and shorter histories; treat
  gaps as unavailable, not as zero.

## When the user asks "is this good"

1. State the configured goal if one exists. If none, say so.
2. Give the entity's own trend over an equal prior window.
3. Give the platform's account-level figure for the same window as a within-account comparison.
4. Only then, optionally, an external range with its caveat.
